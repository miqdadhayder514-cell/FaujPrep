# Initial Interview 1: A Clear, Natural Self-Introduction

## The purpose

A useful introduction helps the interviewer understand who you are and gives the conversation an honest starting point. It is not a speech to memorise word for word. Keep it brief, factual, and easy to follow; the interviewer may interrupt or ask follow-up questions.

## A flexible 60- to 90-second structure

1. **Identity and background:** Give your name, home area, and a concise family or schooling detail if relevant.
2. **Education:** State your current or completed qualification, subjects, and one genuine point about your academic journey.
3. **Interests and responsibility:** Mention one or two real activities, roles, skills, or responsibilities. Say what you actually did.
4. **Motivation:** Explain why you are pursuing a commission and what you understand about the responsibility and demands of service.
5. **Close:** Finish naturally and allow the interviewer to choose the next topic.

For example, describe an activity as: "I helped organise our school science display. I coordinated the materials with three classmates, and I learned to plan earlier when one item was delayed." A concrete example is stronger than a string of labels such as "confident, brilliant, and born to lead."

## A sample introduction to adapt

"I am [name] from [city]. I completed [qualification] with [subjects]. Outside class, I [real activity or responsibility]. In that role, I [specific action], which taught me [lesson]. I am applying for a commission because [your real reason], and I understand that service involves discipline, responsibility, and demands on personal life. I would like to develop further in [relevant area]."

Replace every bracket with a true detail, and omit anything you cannot explain under follow-up. This is a structure, not a script to recite.

## Practise the interruption

**Candidate:** "I helped organise our school science display and coordinated the materials."

**Interviewer:** "What exactly did you do?"

**Candidate:** "I made a list of materials, checked who could bring each item, and followed up the day before. One item was late, so we used a safe substitute approved by our teacher."

**Interviewer:** "What would you do differently?"

**Candidate:** "I would confirm the critical items earlier and identify a backup in advance. I learned that coordination includes checking progress, not just assigning tasks."

The trick is to answer the new question, add one concrete detail, and stop. Do not restart the entire introduction.

## Prepare facts, not a script

Review the details on your application: dates, school names, marks, subjects, activities, and family information. If there is a gap, a change of subject, or a disappointing result, be ready to explain it accurately and briefly. Do not invent awards, responsibilities, or stories. Inconsistencies are avoidable and can undermine trust.

Make a one-page personal fact sheet with:

- Your education timeline and subjects.
- Two genuine strengths, each supported by an example.
- One area you are improving and the steps you are taking.
- A meaningful responsibility or setback and what you learned.
- Your reason for applying, expressed in your own words.

## Practise without sounding rehearsed

Record a practice answer once. Check whether it is understandable, relevant, and under 90 seconds. Then practise answering the same question in different words. Ask a family member to interrupt with follow-ups such as "What did you learn?" or "What would you do differently?" Answer the question asked instead of restarting your prepared speech.

## In the room

Greet politely, listen to the full question, and answer directly. If you do not know a fact, say so calmly rather than guessing confidently. If you need a moment, take one. Good eye contact means attentive and natural, not staring. Respectful disagreement is better than pretending to agree with every assumption.

## Reminder

An introduction cannot secure selection. The goal is honest communication and a fair opportunity for the interviewer to understand your actual background.

## Research basis

The [ISSB candidate guidelines](https://issb.gov.pk/guidelines) advise candidates to be honest, straightforward, and frank, and caution against false projection. They do not publish a fixed PMA Long Course 160 initial-interview script or question list.
