# Initial Test 2: A Four-Week Practice Plan

## First, confirm what applies to you

Use the latest call-up instructions and official recruitment guidance to confirm your test venue, reporting time, required documents, eligibility, and current test procedure. Online posts may describe earlier courses or one centre's experience; do not treat them as a promise about your own paper. There is no reliable shortcut based on a claimed "most repeated" list.

## Week 1: Find the gaps

Take a short, untimed diagnostic across the areas you expect to face. Label each missed item as knowledge, method, reading, pacing, or careless error. Select the three largest recurring gaps. Spend most practice time on those, not on the topics you already answer correctly.

## Week 2: Learn one method at a time

Rotate between verbal reasoning, non-verbal reasoning, and confirmed academic subjects. Use small sets of 10-15 questions. After each set, explain why the right answer works and why your chosen distractor fails. For visual sequences, describe changes in shape, position, rotation, and shading separately. For academic problems, write the formula or principle before calculating.

## Week 3: Add controlled timing

Use mixed sets with a timer, but keep the timer generous at first. Answer straightforward items efficiently, mark uncertain items if the interface permits, and return later. Avoid spending most of the session on one difficult puzzle. Afterward, review every guess as well as every wrong answer; a lucky correct guess still exposes a gap.

## Week 4: Simulate and taper

Complete two or three practice sessions using the latest instructions you have, realistic breaks, and no phone interruptions. Do not turn the final week into an all-night cram. Review your error log, essential formulas, and vocabulary. The day before the test, do a short confidence-building review and prepare your logistics.

## A simple daily schedule

- 5 minutes: recall yesterday's key points without looking.
- 20 minutes: one weak skill area.
- 15 minutes: mixed questions or a short timed set.
- 10 minutes: correct answers and update the error log.

Take a rest day or use a light review day each week. Quality and consistency matter more than an exhausting number of questions.

## Test-room decisions

Read directions fully before beginning. Check whether there is negative marking from official instructions; never assume there is or is not. Watch for words such as **not**, **except**, **least**, and units such as minutes versus seconds. If an item is consuming time, make a reasoned choice and move on rather than letting one question disrupt the rest of the paper.

## Use a two-pass method

**Pass 1:** Answer the items you can solve cleanly. If you have no clear first step after roughly 20-30 seconds in practice, mark the item and move on; adjust this guide to the real time limit and interface. **Pass 2:** Return to marked items and use elimination or a short calculation. Do not let a difficult puzzle steal time from several straightforward questions.

### Example: eliminate with bounds before calculating

Question: **What is 19% of 250?** The options are 37.5, 42.5, 47.5, 52.5.

Ten percent is 25 and twenty percent is 50. The answer must be just below 50, which immediately rules out 37.5 and 52.5. One percent is 2.5, so 19% = 20% - 1% = 50 - 2.5 = **47.5**. This mental check is faster and helps catch a misplaced decimal.

### Example: convert a rank to the opposite end

In a group of 40, a candidate is 12th from the top. Rank from the bottom is **40 - 12 + 1 = 29th**. The +1 matters because the candidate is counted in both lists. A quick check: 12 people are at or above the top-side position, leaving 28 below; counting the candidate from the bottom gives 28 + 1 = 29.

### Example: average questions are totals in disguise

Five values average 18; four remaining values average 16. Original total = 5 × 18 = 90. Remaining total = 4 × 16 = 64. Removed value = **90 - 64 = 26**. Translate every average into "total = count × average" before doing anything else.

### Example: check units before using a formula

A car covers 150 km in 3 hours. Speed = 150 ÷ 3 = **50 km/h**. If the time is 30 minutes, first convert it to 0.5 hour; mixing minutes and hours produces a sixfold error. Write the units beside the numbers so they cancel visibly.

## Build a useful mistake log

For every miss, write one short line: **trigger -> correct method -> my check**. Example: "Rank from opposite end -> total - rank + 1 -> test with a 10-person example." Review this before new questions. Repeating the same practice paper without extracting the trigger does not fix the underlying error.

## Final reminder

This plan is a study method, not an official syllabus or a guarantee of success. Follow the current instructions for your own course and centre.
