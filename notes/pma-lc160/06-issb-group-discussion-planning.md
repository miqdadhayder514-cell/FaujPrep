# ISSB Preparation 1: Group Discussion and Planning Exercises

## The goal is effective teamwork

Group tasks are not won by speaking the most or overpowering other candidates. Useful behaviour includes listening, sharing relevant ideas, helping the group understand the task, and moving toward a workable decision. The exact exercises and assessment methods may vary, so follow current official instructions.

## A simple discussion pattern

1. **Understand:** Identify the question, constraints, and what the group must decide.
2. **Contribute:** Offer one clear, relevant point with a reason or example.
3. **Listen:** Refer to another candidate's point accurately before adding or disagreeing.
4. **Build:** Connect useful ideas and invite a quieter teammate to contribute without putting them on the spot.
5. **Conclude:** Summarise the group's agreed direction if useful, while staying open to correction.

Speak early enough to be part of the discussion, but do not interrupt. If your first idea has already been covered, add a new angle or clarify a trade-off rather than repeating it.

## A five-minute planning drill

Take a scenario such as organising a community event with limited time, people, and equipment. Write:

- The objective.
- The fixed constraints and available resources.
- The urgent actions and their order.
- Who should do what, with a reason.
- One risk and a practical backup.

Then explain your plan in under a minute. Ask a partner to introduce a new constraint and practise adapting without blaming anyone.

## Worked planning example

Your group must organise a school event in two hours with six volunteers, one classroom, and limited supplies. Use **goal -> constraints -> priority -> owner -> backup**:

1. Goal: run a safe, on-time event for the expected attendees.
2. Constraints: two hours, six people, one room, fixed supplies.
3. Priority: confirm attendance and safety, arrange the room, prepare the essential materials, then add optional decoration only if time remains.
4. Owners: one person confirms the room and attendance, two set up seating, two prepare materials, and one checks the schedule and helps where needed.
5. Backup: if a supply is missing, simplify the activity rather than delaying the start.

In discussion, say: "Could we first agree on the essential outcome? I suggest we assign the room and materials now, then use spare time for decoration. Does anyone see a safety or timing issue?" This offers direction and invites useful correction.

If another candidate proposes using all volunteers for decoration, counter the idea, not the person: "That would improve the room. My concern is we have not assigned the materials yet. Could two people decorate while the rest complete the essential setup?"

## Disagreement without conflict

Use language like: "That could work if we have enough time. One risk is ____. Could we first ____ and keep your option as a backup?" This acknowledges the other person's idea, identifies a real constraint, and offers a constructive next step.

Do not fake agreement to appear polite. Raise a concern respectfully and explain the evidence. If the group chooses another reasonable plan, support implementation unless a serious safety or task issue remains.

## Common traps

- Treating every prompt as a speech competition.
- Repeating an idea after it has been understood.
- Attacking a person instead of testing an idea.
- Taking over the plan and ignoring the group's resources.
- Staying silent because you are waiting for a perfect contribution.

## Review yourself

After each practice, rate yourself on listening, relevance, clarity, cooperation, and adaptability. Ask your partner for one observed example, not a vague judgement such as "good leadership." Choose one behaviour to improve in the next round.

## Reminder

These are preparation practices, not a scoring rubric or a promise of ISSB success. Use official ISSB guidance for the current process.
